[Kwant] On the numerical implementation of KPM for Kubo-Bastin formula

bishihao＠connect.hku.hk

Sept. 3, 2023
1:50 p.m.

Dear All,       Recently, I have been engaged in coding the kernel polynomial method for the Kubo-Bastin formula with the aim of investigating potential extensions, including GPU or FFT acceleration. As a concrete example, I have chosen the QWZ model, the 2D massive Dirac fermion, to calculate its Hall conductivity. In the stochastic approach, the conventional trace operation is substituted with an expectation value computed over a random phase vector. By leveraging the recursive relation of Chebyshev polynomials, the problem can be simplified to the computation of the inner product of a series of vectors, as depicted in kwant.kpm<https://kwant-project.org/doc/1/reference/kwant.kpm>. Nevertheless, when implementing the code using such a straightforward approach, the Hall conductivity exhibits significant fluctuations and fails to converge, even when increasing the system size, the number of random phase vectors, or the number of Chebyshev moments considered. In comparison, the Hall conductivity obtained from the kwant package demonstrates a robust behavior, exhibiting stability and consistent results in the band gap, as exhibits in the Appendix A. We observe that within the band gap, the Hall conductivity demonstrates perfect quantization. However, in the bulk region, an oscillatory behavior emerges as the number of Chebyshev moments increases.       In an attempt to understand the underlying cause, I read the source code of kwant.kpm. A notable discovery was that instead of utilizing random phase vectors (although they are employed for calculating the density of states when vector_factory=None), the s_factory = kwant.kpm.LocalVectors(syst, where) function actually generates local vectors that represent a state at the center of the system. And it is similar for kwant.kpm.RandomVectors(syst, where). After implementing this modification in my own code, I observed that the Hall conductivity within the band gap became quantized, as expected.       But the puzzle arises because, in the stochastic approach, the trace is obtained by averaging over random phase vectors, which is considered to be a complete sampling, and the self-average effect guarantees convergence. However, in the kwant.kpm implementation, a local state at the center of the disk in coordinate space is employed instead. The question then remains: why does this alternative approach yield accurate results? And why do the random phase vectors not yield valid results?       Attached are my python codes. The FFT implementation I utilized is based on the reference arXiv:2307.09690<https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09690>.
